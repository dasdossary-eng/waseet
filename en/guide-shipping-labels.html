Buyer's Guide — Shipping Labels

The Complete Guide to Shipping Label Sizes Compatible With Courier Systems

With e-commerce growth, the wrong shipping label has become a leading cause of delayed parcels and sorting errors. Here is how to get the size right the first time.

Why label size matters to couriers

Automated sorting systems at courier companies rely on a barcode scanner that expects a fixed print area. A label smaller than required pushes part of the barcode outside the readable zone, while an oversized label can jam an automatic labeling machine or cover other information on the parcel.

Common sizes in the Saudi market

100x150mm Most common for standard e-commerce parcels
100x100mm Small parcels, envelopes, samples
76x130mm or custom Custom internal systems at some distribution centers

The right size for you depends on the printing and sorting system you work with — that's why we always ask customers which courier or printing system they use before supplying, instead of assuming one size fits everyone.

Thermal vs non-thermal

  • Direct thermal labels need no ink — the most common and cost-effective choice for high shipping volumes
  • Non-thermal labels need an ink/ribbon printer, but tolerate longer storage and heat exposure without fading
  • If parcels sit in a hot warehouse before shipping, non-thermal labels are safer against barcode fading

How to avoid compatibility issues

Before any large order, request a trial sample, print a real barcode on it, and scan it with the same reader your courier uses. This simple test saves you a returned shipment over an incompatible label.
